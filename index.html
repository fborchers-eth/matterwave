What is simulated

The condensate wavefunction obeys the 2D Gross–Pitaevskii equation in a round harmonic trap. Lengths are in aho = √(ħ/mω), times in 1/ω, energies in ħω. The ground state with the barriers switched on is found by imaginary-time evolution. Each disconnected part then gets the phase you set.

The barriers model the blue-detuned light sheet that Andrews et al. used to split their sodium cloud. After the hold, the trap and barriers switch off. Interaction energy drives the expansion, and where the parts overlap their phase difference shows as straight fringes.

Temperature and density

  • Condensate fraction is 1 − (T/Tc)³, with kBTc = 25 ħω.
  • Thermal atoms form a Gaussian cloud of width √(kBT/mω²) that expands ballistically. They do not interfere, so they wash out the contrast.
  • Phase noise is a simple model of thermal phase fluctuations. It is not a full finite-temperature theory.
  • Density sets g·N. A higher density gives a larger chemical potential μ, a larger cloud, a shorter healing length ξ and a faster expansion.

Where the phase comes from

Two independently prepared condensates have no definite relative phase. Javanainen and Yoo, and Castin and Dalibard, showed that detecting atoms one at a time builds up a definite phase: fringes appear in every run, but at a random position each time. Use Randomize and Detect atoms to see this. The simulation is mean-field, so it gives each part a phase at the start. Repeated random phases give the same outcomes as the measurement picture.

For ²³Na in a 100 Hz trap, 1 aho ≈ 2.1 µm and 1/ω ≈ 1.6 ms.
